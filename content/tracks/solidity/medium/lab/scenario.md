Every morning at Kittiwake Quay the boats come in at dawn and the catch is sold on the quayside
before the cafés open. For years the harbour master ran the auction with a clipboard and a cash
tin: bids shouted across the fish boxes, a buyer outbid at the last second arguing that the
bell had already rung, and the harbour's fee worked out in pencil at the end of the week. The
harbour now issues its own token, Harbour Credit, which the boats and the buyers already use for
mooring fees and ice, and the harbour master wants the auction itself on chain: a boat lists a
box with a reserve price and a closing time, buyers bid in Harbour Credit, a late bid gives
everyone else five more minutes, and when the auction closes the boat gets its money less the
harbour's small fee. Nobody's credit may ever be stuck, nobody may be paid twice, and the fee
promised when a box was listed is the fee that is charged.
