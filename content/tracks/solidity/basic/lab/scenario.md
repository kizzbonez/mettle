The town's community orchestra owns a cupboard of instruments that members borrow between
rehearsals: two cellos, a bassoon, a set of flutes. For years the librarian kept the deposits in
a biscuit tin and the loans in a notebook. Deposits went missing, nobody could say who had the
second cello, and a dented flute led to an argument about how much of a deposit should be kept.
This season the orchestra moves the loans to a contract. The librarian lists each instrument with
its deposit and decides who counts as a member. A member borrows by leaving the deposit in ether;
when the instrument comes back, the librarian checks it in and may keep a fee for damage, never
more than the deposit, and the rest is owed to the member. Nobody is ever sent ether unasked:
members withdraw what they are owed when they choose, so a member whose wallet cannot receive
ether can never block a check-in. At every moment the contract must be able to account for every
wei it holds.
